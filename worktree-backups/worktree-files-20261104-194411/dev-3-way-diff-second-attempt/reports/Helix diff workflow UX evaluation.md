# Helix's diff engine outruns its feedback

The diff workflows on `dev-3-way-diff-second-attempt` have a strong core and an unfinished surface: good enough for a motivated git user today, not yet ready to be a default workflow. Alignment, hunk jumps, scroll sync, picker speed, session restore and the accept-a-side loop all work, and buffer diffs and log-driven ranges rate 4/5 in hands-on use. The weakness is honesty and orientation: nothing on screen names what is being compared, `HEAD~N` fails even though the help promises it, `:diff-commit` reports success on refs that do not resolve, and `Space-m f` can stage conflict markers while reporting success. Merge view is the weakest area: its lower panes stay put and carry no side labels, rebases invert what `o` and `t` appear to mean, and `:bc` on its top pane panics deterministically, which can lose unsaved work. Review also stops short of the baseline that mature tools share, with no next-file key, no staging and no key for reverting a hunk. Most defects trace to two habits, state that exists but is never shown and state that is set before it is validated, so a short fix list would move the scores a long way. The panic, the marker gate and ref handling should be fixed before anyone else is asked to rely on this branch. All scores and severities here are researcher judgement from expert walkthroughs; no user testing was done.

## Two workflows rate 4/5, three rate 2/5, and no user has tried any of them

**No user testing was done.** Every rating and severity in this report is the researchers' judgement, so read them as ranked predictions of what users will hit, not as measurements. One tester assigned the scores below on a scale from 5 (smooth) to 1 (broken or dangerous), after reading the book and keymap, which makes the guidance scores a reconstruction and not a blind first look ([hands-on notes, setup and §8](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md)).

| Workflow | Hands-on rating | What earns it | What holds it back |
|---|---|---|---|
| Changed-file review | 3/5 | Picker, alignment, hunk jumps, sync | No sign a diff opened, no next-file key, renames shown as whole-file additions |
| Refs and commits | 2/5 | `^` forms, tags, branches, hashes | `~N` fails, and bad refs are accepted silently |
| Range from git log lines | 4/5, discoverability 2/5 | The clearest messages on the branch | Needs a hand-made `--oneline` log |
| Buffer-to-buffer | 4/5 | Works outside a repo, with scratch buffers, both sides editable | Status message gives a buffer id |
| Merge conflict | 3/5 | Accepting a side | Static unlabelled panes, dead-end delete/modify, false finish |
| Rebase and other sources | 2/5 | Stash pop and cherry-pick conflicts work | Wording inverted from a rebasing user's view, stale panes |
| Abuse and session robustness | 2/5 | No crash from nested splits or resizing to 30x10 | One panic, comparisons go stale when HEAD moves |
| On-screen guidance | 3/5 at entry, 2/5 inside | `Space-m` lists all 12 actions | Goes quiet once a session starts |

The notes use four incompatible scales, and they rate the same defect differently: closing the user's other panes is High in the [lifecycle notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/diff_viewing_and_lifecycle.md), Medium in the [entry-point notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/entry_points_and_discoverability.md) and S3 in the [merge notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/three_way_merge.md). This report does not average them. Its ordering puts wrong outcomes and data risk ahead of friction, which is also a judgement.

The benchmark note derived 26 heuristics from published tools and never read the branch, so mapping the branch onto them is this report's own call ([benchmark checklist](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/external_benchmark.md)). Items 5 and 11 count as missed because the [command inventory](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/entry_points_and_discoverability.md) lists no such feature and a search of the diff sources finds no folding.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| Must (15) | 1 hunk motion; 4 intra-line diff; 6 editable working side; 12 works from marker files; 22 keyboard targeting | 3 alignment and pane switch; 10 clean exit; 15 accept set; 16 progress; 20 use at 80 columns; 21 colour independence; 23 Helix key fit | 2 next file; 13 base on demand; 14 real side labels | None |
| Should (9) | 17 undoable accepts | 7 act in place; 9 undo stated; 18 honest finish; 24 theme scopes | 5 collapse unchanged text; 8 respect selections; 25 one opt-in session model | 19 language servers in the result pane |

That is **6 met, 12 partial, 7 missed and 1 not tested**, with only 5 of 15 musts fully met. The pattern matters more than the count: most of what is met is mechanics, and most of what is missed or partial is orientation and flow.

Coverage has limits. Language servers were disabled in the hands-on runs, mouse focus changes went untested, `zdiff3` could not be produced on git 2.34.1, timings come from a debug build, and only 10 of 195 themes were run ([hands-on notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md), [lifecycle notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/diff_viewing_and_lifecycle.md)). For disputed points this report read the source at `de51c5ef2`, but it did not re-run `hx`. It cites the notes because the 37 saved screen captures sit in a session scratch directory that may not persist.

## Diff review works well until the user asks what they are looking at

The core loop is strong. Split view pads insertions and deletions into alignment, `]g` and `[g` land both panes on each hunk, and sync held for paging, `j`, `ge`, search and the wheel over the focused pane. Character-level highlighting is on by default, working-pane edits update the diff live, returning through `Space-b`, `ga` or `C-o` restores a comparison, and a failed open keeps the current diff. Scale looks unproblematic so far: in a debug build, both a 200,000-line file and a repository with 1,800 changed files filled the picker in about 0.5s, by rough timing ([hands-on §1](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md), [lifecycle notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/diff_viewing_and_lifecycle.md)).

Identity is the first failure. **The default open changes nothing visible**: the screen after `Space-g` then Enter matches normal editing, and removed lines stay out of sight until `Space-m v`, because `editor.diff.split-view` defaults to false and is documented nowhere ([hands-on §1](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md), [entry-point notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/entry_points_and_discoverability.md)). Once split, the left pane is titled `~/.cache/helix/git-rev/5514ae60eedd4174/main.py`, never the `filename @ ref` the [book](../book/src/diff-and-merge.md) promises, and a range diff shows hashes on both sides and no ref anywhere. The right text exists: the statusline simply reads `relative_path()` and not `display_name()` ([lifecycle notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/diff_viewing_and_lifecycle.md)).

The user carries the rest in their head. The lifecycle audit counts **five layers of mostly invisible state**: gutter base, global picker range, comparison session, up to 32 suspended sessions, and a layout preference that survives `:diff-reset` and only a restart clears ([lifecycle notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/diff_viewing_and_lifecycle.md), [entry-point notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/entry_points_and_discoverability.md)). Read-only panes add to the confusion: `i` enters `INS`, and typed text vanishes with no message ([hands-on §1](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md)).

The review loop is the second failure. No next-file key exists, and `]g` stops silently at the last hunk. Stepping costs 4 presses per file in the working tree, but in a range the picker cursor resets to the top because the current path is a cache path, so **19 steps through 20 files cost 247 presses**, or 76 through the undocumented `Space '`. A next-file binding would cost about 2 per file, and `last_changed_file_selection` is already written and never read ([lifecycle notes, journeys](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/diff_viewing_and_lifecycle.md)). Renames are worse than noisy: a `git mv` plus a one-line edit appears as 40 added lines against an empty base. Acting on a review is thin too, since there is no staging command, hunk revert exists only as `:diffget` with no key and no mention on the diff page, and binary files open as garbled text with no warning (same notes).

Sync also has holes. It copies viewports and not cursors, so after `C-d` four times left tops at 82/72, `C-w h` moved both panes to 16/16 and `C-w l` gave 45/45, not the original place. A goto-line command does not sync until the next key, the wheel over the unfocused pane does nothing, and soft wrap drifts rows for the rest of the file ([lifecycle notes, navigation](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/diff_viewing_and_lifecycle.md)). Maintainer archseer called the same kind of jump "disorientating" when reviewing a different side-by-side implementation ([PR #15631](https://github.com/helix-editor/helix/pull/15631)).

## Setup commands report success they never achieved

This is the most misleading part of the branch, and it sits where new users start. **`~N` fails across the board** because the resolver accepts only ref names, hex prefixes and trailing `^` ([git.rs:570-614](../helix-vcs/src/git.rs#L570)). `:diff-base HEAD~1` at least errors, although its help text names `HEAD~N` as valid and the error calls a valid revision invalid ([typed.rs:3477](../helix-term/src/commands/typed.rs#L3477)). The range commands are worse. `:diff-commit nosuchref` prints `Diff range set to: nosuchref — use space+g to browse files`, then the picker lists only untracked files, which looks like a working result. `:diff-commit HEAD~3..HEAD~1` gives an empty picker although `git diff --name-only` lists two files, and `:diff-commit main...feature` also comes up empty ([hands-on §2](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md)). The first honest error appears two steps later, on Enter ([entry-point notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/entry_points_and_discoverability.md)).

The harm is concrete. A user who types `:diff-commit HEAD~3..HEAD` would reasonably conclude those commits changed nothing. In one early run, Enter on an empty picker did nothing, so the next keystrokes edited and saved the real `src/main.py` while the tester believed they were in a read-only snapshot ([hands-on §2](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md)).

The cause is shared. `:diff-commit` checks only the string's shape ([editor.rs:1207-1233](../helix-view/src/editor.rs#L1207)), and listing errors are discarded with `let _ =`, which by code comparison is a regression from master ([entry-point notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/entry_points_and_discoverability.md), [lifecycle notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/diff_viewing_and_lifecycle.md)). Sibling false successes sit nearby: `:diff-base HEAD^` inside a split reports success with no effect, `:diffget` in a read-only pane reports `Reset 1 change` and changes nothing, and `Space-m r` in a merge view reports `Diff reset to HEAD` while the merge stays open ([hands-on §2 and §7](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md), [entry-point notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/entry_points_and_discoverability.md)).

Good messages show what the standard could be. `Diff set: ed5fa5c (HEAD) vs working tree: uncommitted changes only, space m C shows the commit` explains a surprise at the moment it matters, and `Conflict 2/3` orients the user ([hands-on §3 and §8](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md)). The docs have the same split: the key tables are accurate, while the prose is wrong about `filename @ ref`, `HEAD~N`, renamed files in the picker, the scope of `:diff-files`, and the `Space-m f` guard ([entry-point notes, docs audit](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/entry_points_and_discoverability.md)).

## Merge view is a sound marker editor with two unhelpful panes

The design is right. "Buffer is the only state" means accepts and hand edits are the same kind of operation, half-finished work persists as ordinary git markers, and nothing can drift from a hidden hunk model ([merge notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/three_way_merge.md)). That fits the clearest natural experiment in the benchmark: VS Code made its rich merge editor the default, then reversed course because it had "underestimated how good the previous inline conflict experience is" ([#160806](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/160806)). In practice, `x conflict` rows open the view directly, accepts work from any pane, each accept from the top pane undoes in one step, diff3 and CRLF files work, and only `Space-m f` touches the index ([hands-on §5](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md), [merge notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/three_way_merge.md)).

The panes around that editor do not help yet. **With the top pane on line 146, both lower panes stayed on lines 1-19**: the `sync_scroll` field is set and never read. Those panes are identified only by hash paths, with the `HEAD` and `incoming` labels stored but never rendered, and at 80 columns the two titles are identical ([hands-on §5 and §7](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md), [merge notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/three_way_merge.md)). On a long file the user reads the inline markers, as they would without a merge view. The flow also leaves structure to the user. The view opens at 1:1 with no conflict count, accepting does not advance, and `Space-m` is not sticky, so a file with N conflicts costs **6N + 9 keys, or 27 for three** ([merge notes, journey](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/three_way_merge.md)).

The missing base needs a more measured reading than the merge notes give it. They rate its absence S1, as defeating the feature's purpose. The benchmark found no winning layout, and two sides plus a result with base one key away is where VS Code and diffview ended up ([benchmark notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/external_benchmark.md)). So the current layout is defensible, and the real gap is narrower: base is unreachable unless `merge.conflictStyle=diff3` puts it inline, and even then no accept key takes it. That is a must-fix for parity, but less urgent than labels and following panes. The name also oversells the feature, since this is two sides plus a result.

`Space-m f` is the highest-stakes key and has the weakest check. It counts only paired `<<<<<<<` and `>>>>>>>` regions ([merge_view.rs:43-63](../helix-view/src/merge_view.rs#L43)), so deleting one marker line by hand leaves the rest to be **staged with `File saved and staged (git add)`**. The book says this case produces an error ([diff-and-merge.md:94-95](../book/src/diff-and-merge.md#L94)). Real content can cause the same result: a `>>>>>>>` line inside a conflict side ends the region early, an accept drops lines, and a real marker then stages ([merge notes, edge cases](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/three_way_merge.md)). The key also works on any file, so a slip stages partial work, including unstaged hunks of an `MM` file, with no prompt ([hands-on §5](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md)).

Rebases expose the rest. `Space-m o` reports `Accepted HEAD version` and takes upstream, while `Space-m t` takes the user's own commit. The wording is literally true, and the `o` and `t` mnemonics still point the wrong way for someone thinking "mine" and "theirs" ([hands-on §6](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md)). Git itself documents that the sides are swapped ([git-rebase](https://git-scm.com/docs/git-rebase)), and VS Code still carries open bugs on the same point ([#247687](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/247687)). A multi-commit rebase is worse: with the view left open across `git rebase --continue`, the lower panes kept the previous commit, and the `theirs` pane lacked the change on screen. Closing and reopening fixes it, but stale panes look authoritative ([hands-on §6](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md)). Marker-less conflicts are dead ends, and finishing happens outside the editor: nothing offers commit or continue, and `:sh git rebase --continue` failed in the one unset-editor case tried ([merge notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/three_way_merge.md)).

The commit history explains the gap. The recent fixes for session preservation and failed opens covered diffs and stopped short of merge, which remains near its first working state ([merge notes, design intent](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/three_way_merge.md)).

## One deterministic panic survives, in merge view and not in diff sessions

**`:bc` on merge view's top pane crashes `hx` with exit code 101.** The trigger matrix also covers `:bc!`, `:buffer-close-all` and `:write-buffer-close`. `:bc` with unsaved edits in that pane is refused, and closing either lower pane is safe. The crash depends on the pane's history and needs a pane with no earlier buffer, which is the state right after `hx file` then `:merge`. Git state survives, but **typed text in another buffer never reached disk** in the test built to check that ([hands-on §7](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md)).

The code explains both the crash and its limits. `close_document` ends diff sessions up front but not merge sessions ([editor.rs:2173-2186](../helix-view/src/editor.rs#L2173)). With no earlier buffer it calls `close`, which ends the merge session and deliberately keeps the last view so that `:q` does not quit ([editor.rs:2149](../helix-view/src/editor.rs#L2149)). That view still points at the document `close_document` then removes, and `_refresh` unwraps a missing document at [editor.rs:1869](../helix-view/src/editor.rs#L1869), matching the saved backtrace. This trace is from reading and was not re-run. Commit `9988007b4` fixed `:bc` panics, but its two tests cover merge side panes and range-diff panes, not the top pane.

The session weakness that matters most after the panic is staleness. Open comparisons ignore HEAD: after a commit in another window, a split kept its old hunks through `:w` and `:reload`, and re-selecting the file was the fix. `git merge --abort` gave no notice either, and `Space-m o` still reported `Accepted HEAD version` on the stale buffer ([hands-on §7](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md)). A snapshot that stays fixed is defensible only if the screen says which commit it is, which leads back to labels. The save guard does hold: writing over an externally changed file is refused, and `git add` does not run ([merge notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/three_way_merge.md)).

Layout damage is the other lasting cost. Entering split or merge closes every other pane with no warning and no restore, and opening straight into split also loses the pane's jumplist and alternate file ([lifecycle notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/diff_viewing_and_lifecycle.md)). Neighbouring problems sit outside the diff feature or at its edges. **Statusline overflow is general Helix behaviour, not a diff bug**: a plain `:vsplit` with a long left path shows the same stray characters, and long snapshot paths simply make it likelier, up to overwriting a neighbour's mode label. Theme support is sound in mainstream themes and weaker at the edges: 6 themes get no intraline highlight, 9 get a fixed dark green tint, and 14 lose their own `diff.plus` background ([hands-on §7](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/hands_on_observations.md), [lifecycle notes, themes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/diff_viewing_and_lifecycle.md)).

## Fix order: honesty first, then orientation, then flow

This ordering is this report's judgement, drawing on fixes the notes propose. The suggested routes are untested.

| Order | Fix | What it clears |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | End merge sessions in `close_document` or otherwise stop the top-pane panic, and add a test | The only defect found that destroys unsaved work |
| 2 | Reject any marker-shaped line at `Space-m f`, and decide deliberately how it behaves outside conflicted files | False success one `git commit` from history |
| 3 | Adopt full revision parsing, validate at set time, and report listing errors in the picker | `~N`, `...`, bad refs, non-repo silence. `resolve_commit_id` is a ready hook, though `gix` `revision` availability is unconfirmed |
| 4 | Render `display_name()` in the statusline, buffer picker and bufferline, add a session indicator, and default to split or show removed lines | Cache paths, unlabelled sides, "did anything happen?" |
| 5 | Scroll side panes on conflict jump, land on the first conflict, advance after accept, and make `Space-m` sticky | Most of the merge flow's cost |
| 6 | Refresh or flag comparisons when HEAD moves | Stale panes in `git commit`, `rebase --continue` and `merge --abort`, which may share a cause |
| 7 | Add next-file and hunk-revert keys | The largest review friction |
| 8 | Name marker-less conflicts, resolve renames for the chosen file, restore layout on exit, and correct the book | Remaining dead ends and false docs |

Items 1 to 3 are small relative to their risk, and item 4 mostly connects text that already exists. Upstream context raises the stakes: side-by-side diffing has 256 thumbs-up on [#405](https://github.com/helix-editor/helix/issues/405) and in-editor conflict resolution has 81 on [#5132](https://github.com/helix-editor/helix/issues/5132). Yet pascalkuthe has said the project is "unlikely to implement 5 different diff modes" ([#9127](https://github.com/helix-editor/helix/issues/9127)), the-mikedavis placed write operations in plugins in 2022 ([#2261](https://github.com/helix-editor/helix/issues/2261)), and two open PRs overlap this scope ([#15631](https://github.com/helix-editor/helix/pull/15631), [#15791](https://github.com/helix-editor/helix/pull/15791)). The branch's separate merge session and its unconditional `Space-g` change, which has no opt-out, are the likeliest points of friction, and VS Code's reversal shows what forced defaults can cost ([#160806](https://github.com/microsoft/vscode/issues/160806)). No maintainer statement on staging or merge views from 2025 or 2026 was found ([benchmark notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/external_benchmark.md)).

## Conclusion

The branch's main debt is one of honesty rather than missing features. Its history shows logic bugs getting structural fixes while feedback bugs got local ones, so the next reports are likely to come from the same silent-failure and sync classes ([lifecycle notes](../research_notes/Helix%20diff%20workflow%20UX%20evaluation/diff_viewing_and_lifecycle.md)). Two standing rules would address the class: no command reports success before its state is validated, and no session exists without a visible name. A practitioner survey, dated but the closest evidence available, points the same way: ease of understanding conflicting code ranks first at 3.89 of 5, ahead of tools presenting understandable information at 3.48 ([Nelson et al. 2019](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~sarmaa/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Nelson2019_Article_TheLife-cycleOfMergeConflictsP.pdf)). For this branch, that argues for labels and panes that follow the conflict ahead of a larger accept set.

The biggest unknown is what real users do. The judgements most exposed to a usability test are also the ones the tester could only infer: whether the single-pane default reads as nothing happening, whether `o` misleads during a rebase, and whether `HEAD~N` is the first thing people type. A short session with a few users on those three tasks, after the panic and marker fixes, would do more to settle priorities than another expert pass.
